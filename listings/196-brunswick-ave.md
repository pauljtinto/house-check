# 196 Brunswick Ave — evaluation

**Verdict: STRETCH — and the diligence can't be done in time.**

MLS C13702882 · List $1,390,000 · DOM 8 · Detached, 2½ storey · 18 × 120 ft lot · 2+2 bed, 3 bath ·
1,500–2,000 sqft · Taxes **$12,622.26** (2026) · **Offers Sep 2 at 8pm, pre-emptive offers accepted**

---

## 1. The ask is not the price

Offers are being held to a date. That is a hold-back, so $1,390,000 is a marketing decision.

Three independent signals point the same way:

| Signal | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Detached in this pocket** | The only two detached comps sold at **$1,850,000** and **$3,000,000**. Detached median $2,425,000. A detached listed at $1.39M is far below where detached trades here. |
| **Offer night declared** | Every hold-back in the comp set sold over ask. Nearer $1M the premium ran +19.7% to +42.0%; at $1.4–1.7M the two observed over-ask sales ran +5.9% and +6.8%. |
| **Property tax** | $12,622.26 at Toronto's 2026 rate of 0.767311% implies an MPAC assessed value of **~$1,645,000** — and that is on a **2016** valuation basis, **18.3% above the asking price**. Assessments here normally sit *well below* today's prices. |

**Modelled landing: $1,598,500, range $1,472,000 – $1,792,000.** Low confidence, n=2 — this is a warning
that the ask is wrong, not a forecast.

That tax number is the one to sit with. If MPAC really valued this at $1.645M as of 2016, today's value is
plausibly north of $2M and the ask is a very aggressive hold-back. **But verify before believing it** — a gut
renovation triggers reassessment, and a local improvement charge on the bill would distort it. Ask the agent
directly what the assessed value is.

**Either way: $1.39M is not the number. Budget $1.55M–$1.75M to be competitive, and possibly more.**

---

## 2. What passes

**Bikeability: 93/100 — and 93/100 if the Bill 212 lanes go.** The best possible result on your hard gate.
Steps from Harbord, which is the concrete cycle track completed Nov 2025 and *not* named in Bill 212. South
of Davenport, so no escarpment. And the **detached garage is secure bike storage** — the property-level
factor that decides whether you actually ride daily. Zero resilience gap. Nothing in the Bloor fight touches
this address.

**This is the sixplex property.** Every previous listing we looked at was a semi or row house, capped at four
units. This is **detached in Ward 11** — the only form the June 2025 five-and-six-unit permission reaches. Add
lane access and a 120 ft lot, and the growth pillar has real option value here that your budget could not buy
in any semi. That is the genuine case for stretching.

**Near-ready in-law suite.** Finished basement, **separate entrance**, bedroom, 3-piece bath, and a
**rough-in for a second kitchen**. Rec room 18.3 × 14.2. Not income today — a rough-in is not a kitchen — but
the expensive parts are done.

**Solar income ~$3,300 in 2025.** Roughly $275/month, if it transfers.

---

## 3. What doesn't

**Two bedrooms above grade.** That is the real problem. 2+2 in 1,500–2,000 sqft, on an **18 ft frontage** —
semi-width, on a detached lot. They spent the space on a 22 × 16 primary with 16-foot vaulted ceilings and a
floating glass staircase. Dramatic, and it costs you a bedroom. Two home offices plus guest space means
putting someone in the basement. Two-bedroom detached is also a narrower resale market than the price
implies.

**It reads as a flip.** Vacant, "newly renovated", digitally staged photography, **no survey**, sold by a name
that isn't a long-term resident pattern. The 16-foot vault means the ceiling structure was opened up — that is
structural work. **Ask for the building permits and closed inspections on the renovation.** Unpermitted
structural work behind new finishes is the expensive kind of surprise.

**Lease-to-own furnace and A/C.** $938.04 + HST and $1,030 + HST per year — about **$2,224/year**, and
lease-to-own contracts typically carry punishing buyouts. **Get the buyout figures before you offer.** This is
the line most buyers skim past.

**Taxes are $1,052/month.** Whatever the assessment story is, that is what you pay, and it is high.

**Heritage unconfirmed.** MLS says "Spec Desig: Unknown", which means the agent left it blank, not that it's
clear. 196 Brunswick sits north of Ulster, outside Harbord Village HCD Phase 1 (nos. 10–88 west / 15–101
east) — so **probably clear** — but West Annex HCD Phase II is a live study. Run the address through the
City Heritage Property Search.

## 5. The thing that actually decides it

**Offers are Sunday at 8pm. Pre-emptive offers are being accepted.** It could sell before then.

The diligence this property needs — renovation permits, the lease buyout figures, the solar contract terms
and remaining years, heritage confirmation, a survey, basement ceiling height for suite legality, and a real
inspection of structural work behind new drywall — **cannot be done in two days.** And on offer night a
conditional offer loses to a firm one.

So the honest framing is not "is this worth $1.6M." It is: **are you willing to go firm at $1.55M–$1.75M on a
recently gutted flip with no survey, unverified permits, and an undisclosed lease obligation?**

For a two-bedroom house.

**My read: let this one go, and use it.** It is an excellent calibration data point — watch what it actually
sells for on Sep 2, then feed the result back into the model. If it goes at $1.5M you have learned the
detached premium is softer than the comps suggest and your ceiling reaches further than we thought. If it
goes at $1.75M you have learned that detached in Harbord Village is out of range, and you should stop
looking at them.

**If you do want to pursue it,** the sequence is: call the agent today for the assessed value, the lease
buyout figures, the solar contract, and the permit list; get your broker to confirm the approved mortgage
amount tonight; and decide a walk-away number *before* Sunday rather than during.

## 6. What this listing taught the model

The classifier keyed off list price alone, so it read $1.39M as "priced to market" and predicted a landing
*below* ask — comfortably inside your ceiling. That was wrong, and it would have been a costly kind of wrong.

Fixed: a **hold-back flag** now overrides the price band, because a declared offer date *is* the signal the
band was only a proxy for. With it on, the same listing predicts $1,598,500 and the price gate flips from
pass to warn. There's a new checkbox in the app, and three tests covering it. 52 tests passing.

The remaining known gap: **only 2 of 23 comps are detached**, so the model has almost no basis for pricing
detached property. Worth asking your agent for detached-only solds in C01 under $2M.

Median **+6.4%**, and a spread of less than one percentage point across three sales spanning $1.35M–$1.70M.
That is a far better-supported number than the +15% midpoint I used yesterday, which was a guess splitting
the difference between this cluster and the +29% seen nearer $1M.

**Recalibrated, 196 Brunswick predicts $1,478,960 rather than $1,598,500** — which puts it *inside* your
$1.5M ceiling rather than $98,500 over it. The price gate flips from warn to pass.

**But I am not withdrawing the recommendation, for three reasons.**

1. **The tax-implied assessment still points the other way.** $12,622.26 implies an MPAC value near
   $1,645,000 on a 2016 basis. If that is accurate, $1.39M is a much more aggressive hold-back than a
   routine +6.4%, and the three calibration comps — a duplex and two semis — may not describe a detached
   Victorian that MPAC values above its own asking price.

2. **All three calibration comps are semis or a duplex.** We still have almost no detached data. The two
   detached comps in the set sold at $1.85M and $3.0M. The recalibration makes the model better at pricing
   the kind of property it has seen, and no better at pricing this one.

3. **Nothing about the diligence problem changed.** Permits on structural work, the lease-to-own buyouts,
   the solar contract, heritage confirmation, a survey, basement ceiling height — still undoable before
   Sunday, and still not something to waive on a recently gutted flip.

So the honest revision is: **the price is more likely reachable than I said, and the reasons to be careful
are unchanged.** If you were going to pursue it, this makes it more plausible, not less risky. Watch what it
actually sells for on Sep 2 — with the recalibrated model that is now a genuine test rather than a guess.
