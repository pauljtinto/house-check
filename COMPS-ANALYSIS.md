# Comps Analysis — Toronto C01 "University" (Harbord Village)

**Source:** MLS sold report prepared by Joseph Tucci, Sutton Group–Associates Realty, printed 2026-08-29.
**Set:** 23 sold properties, all Toronto C01 / University community. Contract dates Mar–Aug 2026.
**Data file:** `data/comps-university-c01.csv`

**Transcription check:** the 23 sold prices I transcribed produce a mean of **$1,653,817** and a median of **$1,500,000** — matching the report's own stated figures exactly. The data below is reliable.

---

## The headline: list price in this market is a strategy, not a signal

The comps split into two completely different pricing behaviours, and blending them produces a number that describes no actual house.

| List band | n | Median sold/list | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Under $1.25M** | 7 | **128.9%** | 96.0 – 142.0% |
| **$1.25M – $1.5M** | 3 | 91.7% | 90.7 – 106.8% |
| **$1.5M+** | 13 | **94.6%** | 91.1 – 105.9% |

The underpricing band is tight and remarkably consistent. **Every property listed between $989K and $1.199M sold 20–42% over ask:**

| Property | List | Sold | Over ask | Days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 627 Bathurst St | $989,000 | $1,315,000 | +33.0% | 7 |
| 81B Ulster St | $995,000 | $1,320,000 | +32.7% | 7 |
| 91 Borden St | $999,000 | $1,195,800 | +19.7% | 11 |
| 138 Major St | $999,000 | $1,419,000 | +42.0% | 7 |
| 63 Sussex Ave | $1,199,000 | $1,546,000 | +28.9% | 10 |

Five for five. Median landing zone: **$1,320,000**.

**What this means practically:** a Harbord Village house listed at $999K is not a $999K house. It is a $1.2M–$1.55M house wearing an offer-night price tag. You should absolutely be watching sub-$1.2M listings — but budgeting $1.3M–$1.55M to win one, and treating the list price as noise.

The mirror image is just as useful. Anything listed **above $1.5M sells at a median 94.6% of ask** — roughly 5% of built-in negotiating room. Those are the listings where an offer below asking is normal rather than insulting.

*(Two exceptions worth noting: 176 Brunswick listed at $849K and sold at 96% — the cheapest sale in the set by $380K, which suggests a property with a real problem. And 60 Ulster at $1,238K sold at 98%. The pattern holds in the $950K–$1.2M band specifically, not simply "anything cheap.")*

## Speed tells you the answer before the price does

| Days on market | n | Median sold/list |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 11 days | 10 | **113.3%** |
| ≥ 30 days | 10 | **93.4%** |

A 20-point swing. In this market, days-on-market is the single best predictor of negotiating room, and it's available before you write an offer. Anything sitting past 30 days is signalling that the ask was wrong — 17 Major St went 61 days and sold at 90.7%; 266 Brunswick went 62 days and sold at 91.1%.

This should be a first-class input to the app's price-fairness test, not a footnote.

## What $1.5M actually buys here

**12 of 23 sales (52%) closed at or under $1.5M.** Every one of them was a semi or a row house:

- **Semi-detached: 7** — $815K to $1.5M
- **Att/Row/Townhouse: 5** — $1,195,800 to $1.48M
- **Detached: 0**

Median sold price by type across the whole set:

| Type | n | Median | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semi-Detached | 13 | **$1,500,000** | $815K – $2,150,000 |
| Att/Row/Twnhouse | 7 | $1,419,000 | $1,195,800 – $2,450,000 |
| Detached | 2 | $2,425,000 | $1,850,000 – $3,000,000 |
| Triplex | 1 | $2,600,000 | — |

Two conclusions, both worth sitting with:

1. **Your $1.5M pre-approval buys almost exactly the median semi in Harbord Village.** Not the top of the market, not a compromise — the middle. That's a comfortable, realistic position.
2. **Detached is out of reach here.** The cheapest detached in the set sold at $1,850,000 — $350K above your ceiling, and that was a 3-storey on Lippincott. If detached matters, either the budget has to stretch well past the pre-approval or the neighbourhood has to change. If it doesn't matter, this is a non-issue and the semi market is deep.

## Income potential is normal here, not exotic

**9 of 23 properties (39%) had two or more kitchens** — the MLS proxy for an existing second suite or duplex conversion. That's an unusually high rate and it confirms the income pillar is live in this neighbourhood rather than theoretical.

Four of them sold within your budget:

- 627 Bathurst St — $1,315,000, 4BR/3WR, 2 kitchens
- 91 Borden St — $1,195,800, 5+1BR/3WR, 2 kitchens
- 138 Major St — $1,419,000, 4+1BR/3WR, 2 kitchens
- 224 Lippincott St — $1,492,000, 4+1BR/3WR, 2 kitchens

So a duplex-capable property inside the budget is a realistic target, not a stretch. **The critical unknown is legality** — a second kitchen in the MLS says nothing about whether the suite is legal (egress, ceiling height, fire separation). That distinction is worth a large amount of money and the MLS data cannot answer it. It goes on the per-listing checklist.

Note also that 69 Brunswick — a legal triplex — sold at $2.6M. There's a visible premium for legitimacy at the top of this market.

## Two pieces of good news on bikeability

Every address in this set is Harbord Village: Brunswick, Borden, Major, Lippincott, Robert, Sussex, Ulster, Willcocks, Bathurst.

1. **This is the Harbord bike lane corridor.** Your bikeability gate is satisfied by the Harbord/Hoskin spine, which is independent of the contested Bloor lanes. The bike-lane removal risk flagged in the plan is much less threatening for this specific pocket than for the Annex proper north of Bloor.
2. **It's flat.** All of this sits south of Davenport, so the Lake Iroquois escarpment climb never enters the daily ride.

Harbord Village may be the single best answer to your stated constraints. Which makes the next item more important.

## ⚠️ Heritage — the thing to check before falling in love

Much of Harbord Village sits within a Heritage Conservation District, and individual properties may be separately listed or designated. That constrains exterior alteration, additions and third-storey work — which directly reduces the growth pillar (expandability) and can complicate the income pillar (laneway suites, additions).

Because **23 of 23 comps are in this area**, this is not a per-listing curiosity — it's a structural feature of the neighbourhood you're most likely to buy in. Phase 0 should establish the exact HCD boundaries and the designation status lookup, and every listing should be checked individually before an offer.

## What this data doesn't have

Three gaps, all of which affect the model:

1. **No square footage.** No $/sqft analysis is possible from this export. Sqft has to come from full listings, GeoWarehouse, or MPAC. The app must treat sqft as optional and fall back to price-per-bedroom and price-per-frontage when it's missing.
2. **No lot dimensions.** This is the bigger loss. Lot size and frontage drive the land-share ratio and laneway-suite feasibility — the two most valuable inputs to the growth and income pillars. Worth requesting specifically.
3. **No Trinity Bellwoods.** You named the Annex, U of T and Trinity Bellwoods; this set is University community only. Trinity Bellwoods trades as a different market and needs its own comp set before the app can compare across your three target areas.

---

## What this changes in the plan

1. **Add a list-price-strategy classifier.** Before anything else, the app must decide whether a listing is priced for offer night or priced to sell, and predict the landing price accordingly. Without this, every fair-value estimate in this market is wrong by up to 40%.
2. **Promote days-on-market to a primary input** of the price-fairness test, with the ≤11 / ≥30 day split as the starting rule.
3. **Segment comps by property type before averaging** — confirmed necessary, since semi and detached medians differ by ~$925K here.
4. **Set the stretch band from data.** Semis have a real market up to ~$2.15M in this pocket, but the median is $1.5M. A stretch limit somewhere around $1.65M–$1.75M would buy meaningfully above the median without chasing the top of the market. Your call, but the data now supports a number rather than a guess.
5. **Second-kitchen flag** on every listing, paired with a mandatory "is it legal?" check.
6. **Heritage status lookup** becomes a phase 0 requirement, not a phase 7 nicety.
